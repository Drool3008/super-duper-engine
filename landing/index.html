How it works

1

Add your family

Install the app, add your parents, photograph one prescription. That is the setup.

2

It counts the days

It knows the dose and how many tablets were actually dispensed, so the days-left number is real, not an estimate.

3

It makes the call

It phones the clinic, sits through the hold, and hands your parent the line when a person picks up.

4

You only hear about money

Anything under your limit just happens. Anything above it waits in your wallet for a yes.

Your parent keeps every decision.

Twelve of the twenty-five people we surveyed said their parent no longer phones the clinic at all. A child does it now: books the slot, orders the medicines, answers the doctor's questions for them.


But when we asked the parents, five of nine said they still want to be asked first. They gave up the errands. They never gave up the choices.

So Vantari takes the phone call. Never the room.
"History gets lost a lot if someone new is handling."

What the whole family sees

Every action appears in the family view, so the sibling who does nothing still knows the tablets arrived and who received them. Anyone can ask in the chat: when was the last sugar test, what is he taking now, send me the reports. And get an answer without phoning the one person who always knows.
